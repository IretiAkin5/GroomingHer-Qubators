# GroomingHer Design Plan

Version 1.0 · 6 October 2026 · Based on PRD version 3.0

The first audience is Nigerian girls aged 13–15, supported by parents/guardians and participating Christian schools.

This plan describes the proposed experience and visual direction for the first web app. It follows the approved education-first scope. It contains no technical specifications and does not represent completed designs. Visual choices are recommendations for founder and user review.

## 1. Design goal

Make GroomingHer feel welcoming, trustworthy and age-appropriate: a place where a girl can learn without embarrassment, a parent can find practical guidance, and school staff can deliver reviewed sessions. Christian identity should be clear while health education remains easy to find.

### Principles

- Speak directly and kindly. Use everyday language with accurate body terms explained simply.
- Respect girls as learners; avoid designs that feel babyish or suggest shame.
- Give learning and support priority over tracking.
- Make privacy boundaries visible where a person makes a decision.
- Keep faith reflections identifiable and connected to practical compassion.
- Offer a complete core learning experience through school sessions and print.
- Explain limits and unsuccessful actions honestly.

## 2. Proposed visual direction

No GroomingHer brand palette has been confirmed. The palette below is a starting recommendation, not an adoption of the founder’s personal portfolio colours.

| Element | Proposed direction | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main colour | Deep plum, #642C58 | Identity, headings and primary actions. |
| Background | Warm off-white, #FFF9F4 | Calm reading surface. |
| Supporting colour | Pale lavender, #F0E8F5 | Lesson and reflection backgrounds. |
| Supporting colour | Soft peach, #FCE5DC | Gentle illustrations and section accents. |
| Main text | Dark charcoal, #25222A | Readable body text. |
| Support accent | Deep teal, #195D59 | Support links and selected guidance. |
| Status colours | Distinct colours plus text/icon labels | Show pending, successful, unavailable and urgent states without relying on colour alone. |

Check contrast on every actual text/background combination before approval. Use one familiar, readable sans-serif typeface such as system sans-serif; keep paragraphs short, line spacing comfortable and text resizable. Rounded cards may soften the experience, but avoid decorative clutter.

### Imagery

Use respectful illustrations of Nigerian girls with varied skin tones, hairstyles and everyday clothing, together with supportive adults and school learning scenes. Girls must appear age-appropriate, with no sexualised poses or body emphasis. Anatomy illustrations should be medically reviewed and used where they teach something clearly. Avoid imagery implying all Nigerian families or schools look the same.

Christian identity can appear through short, clearly labelled reflections and scripture. Avoid showing faith as a measure of health or using religious symbols as medical credibility badges.

## 6. Onboarding design

Use short steps with a visible progress indicator and a Back action. Explain why each required detail is needed. Optional preferences have Skip. Separate consent from learning preferences. Do not use onboarding to collect symptom histories.

| Role | Screen sequence | Main questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Girl | Invitation → welcome → age/name → learning/access preferences → permission and agreement → connection → welcome home | Preferred name; age 13/14/15; topic interests; own/shared device or printed learning. |
| Parent | Invitation → account → programme/privacy information → preferences → permissions → connection → welcome | Relationship to girl; topics needing support; family access method. Relationship selection alone does not establish authority. |
| School | Interest form → pending response → readiness discussion → approved invitation → staff account → readiness home | School/city/state; representative and reply route; eligible group size; ability to support sessions/printing; Christian approach; available female support contact. |

### Important onboarding states

- Invalid invitation: explain the issue and provide help without revealing participant lists.
- Permissions pending: show what remains and keep public learning available.
- Outside pilot age: explain current eligibility; offer public resources.
- Wrong connection: prevent linking and provide correction support.
- Interrupted journey: return to the last completed step without silently confirming choices.
- School interest submitted: show pending status, with no promised approval/date.

## 7. Girl screens

| Screen | Design breakdown |
| --- | --- |
| Home | Friendly welcome; current lesson; next learning action; resources and support. Do not display personal symptoms on the summary. |
| My Lessons | Six topic cards with completion status. Lesson detail: objective → explanation → illustration → distinct Christian reflection → fictional activity → optional check → next step. |
| Resources | Topic list, reviewed article cards and related lessons; the same factual content as public resources. |
| Ask GroomingHer | Education-scope explanation; suggested lesson questions; text entry; answer with source; report answer; human-help link. No diary import or intimate-image attachment. |
| Learning check | One fictional scenario, options, respectful explanation and retry/skip. No grade, ranking or compulsory personal disclosure. |
| Diary | Date; selected period details; optional flow, pain, impact, symptoms/note; Save and Cancel. Plain explanation of privacy and optional use. |
| Diary history | Recorded dates and entries; open/edit/delete; clear empty state. No automatic interpretation or prediction. |
| Help Me Tell Someone | Verified recipient → editable suggested or own message → exact preview → Send/Cancel → truthful delivery status. |
| Shared messages | Recipient, date, exact sent text and status. Explain that delivered does not mean read or acted on. |
| Get Support | Routine contact, availability, backup and reviewed urgent route. Always reachable; no claim of continuous monitoring. |
| Progress | Completed/remaining learning only; no diary streaks or health score. |
| Account/privacy | Connections, pause future sharing, report wrong pairing, correction/deletion request and visible logout. |

When first opening the diary, use a fictional demonstration with no actual health data. If a girl chooses not to track, return her to learning without warnings or loss of access.

## 9. School and management screens

School views should feel organised and practical. Use simple schedules and task lists rather than health-monitoring dashboards.

| School screen | Design breakdown |
| --- | --- |
| Programme | Schedule, named responsibilities and readiness tasks. |
| Sessions | Six topics, dates, facilitator and approved changes. |
| Materials | Current facilitator guides, girl lessons, parent sheets and blank diaries; show version/review information. |
| Participation | Invitations and required joining status only. Optional diary use stays private. |
| Attendance | Session roster and attendance corrections; no health reasons for absence. |
| Support | Verified contact, hours, backup and referral arrangements. |
| Questions guidance | Instructions for a physical anonymous box, review timing and separate urgent-help route. |
| Results/feedback | Combined learning/confidence feedback, counts and response rates; suppress identifiable small groups. |
| Staff settings | Named authorised accounts and access based on duties; revoke access when staff leave. |

Management needs content drafts, review decisions, approved school records, programme arrangements, reports and privacy requests. Published health content must show its approval/version status. A credible serious report must allow pausing affected content or AI access. Routine management screens must not show private diaries or individual health-risk lists.

## 10. Reusable design elements

Prepare consistent designs for navigation, topic/article cards, lesson sections, reflection panels, questions, role cards, invitations, form fields, permission summaries, message previews, support contacts, schedules, readiness lists and feedback confirmations.

Every relevant action needs an appropriate state: initial/empty, in progress, saved/submitted, unsuccessful, unavailable, pending or withdrawn. Use text explanations with status colours. Distinguish message delivery from read/response. Never use a green health status to imply medical clearance.

## 11. Accessibility, shared devices and print

- Use readable text, strong contrast, visible keyboard focus and clearly labelled form fields.
- Make actions usable without precise tapping; do not rely on colour, hover or icons alone.
- Keep sections short and support normal browser zoom. Review the complete experience on a small phone screen.
- Give illustrations useful text alternatives; decorative images should not distract from reading.
- Avoid sensitive details in notification previews and account summaries.
- Keep logout easy to find; show practical shared-device guidance without promising complete privacy.
- Printed lessons must remain understandable in black and white, include page/topic identification and explain offline support.
- Blank paper diaries are optional and never collected or graded by default.

## 12. Design and validation sequence

| Stage | Outputs | Completion check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Confirm structure | Navigation map, role visibility table, list of required screens. | Founder confirms alignment with PRD. |
| 2. Draft low-detail layouts | Landing/public pages, role selection, onboarding and principal role screens. | Each page shows actual sections/actions without repeated marketing placeholders. |
| 3. Sample lesson experience | Girl lesson, facilitator notes, parent guide, print edition. | Healthcare/faith review before children use the material. |
| 4. Test task journeys | Clickable draft using fictional information. | Girls/parents/staff can understand navigation and sharing boundaries; participation is voluntary with appropriate permissions. |
| 5. Refine visual direction | Palette, typography, illustrations and reusable elements. | Readability, age suitability and founder approval. |
| 6. Finish key states | Permissions pending, wrong pairing, diary save failure, message failure, unavailable AI and urgent support. | No silent sharing, false success or blocked support. |
| 7. Prepare design handoff | Screen inventory, annotated flows, content/review status and matching print materials. | Each designed feature maps to PRD acceptance criteria. |

### Suggested validation tasks

A girl finds a period lesson, skips tracking, edits a fictional diary entry, cancels a message before sending and finds an alternative support contact. A parent finds a guide and explains what remains private. School staff find materials and readiness tasks without viewing personal health details. Participants also explain whether the faith content feels caring and whether wording is clear.

Observe confusion rather than asking for personal medical disclosures. Do not use real diaries or intimate information in prototypes.

## 13. Scope and handoff boundaries

The first design covers a web app with public pages and Girl, Parent, School and management spaces. Mobile-app designs, automated symptom alerts, diagnosis flows, fertility predictions, peer discussions and frequent-blog publishing are outside this first design scope.

Final landing copy will be supplied later. Other functional wording can be drafted during design, with health/privacy/support messages reviewed by the relevant professionals. The designer must mark incomplete content and must not invent WHO approval, secured partners or live clinical services.

Recommended handoff: page layouts, role-based journey flows, essential interaction states, reusable elements, print layouts, accessible design notes and an acceptance-criteria checklist. A functioning build is a later delivery step; this plan is not a completed interface or implementation.
