# GroomingHer

## Product Requirements Document

A Christian teenage health education web app for Nigerian girls aged 13–15 and their parents.

Owner: Ireti Ogunmola | Version: 3.1 | Date: 6 October 2026

Status: Planning draft. Ready for product discussion and partner review; not approved for delivery to children. Health, child protection and privacy arrangements require professional review before the pilot.

This document describes what the product should do and how success will be assessed. It intentionally excludes technical specifications. The founder has approved the feature inventory below. Expanded behaviour and acceptance criteria are recommended details for this approved scope. Proposed landing-page copy is supplied for the first preview; the founder will revise it after seeing the product.

## 1. Purpose and product promise

GroomingHer helps girls understand their changing bodies, recognise when to ask for help and communicate with trusted adults. Christian reflections reinforce dignity, compassion, care for the body and courage. Health education remains the main experience.

Product promise: “Understand your body. Ask questions with confidence. Get support when you need it.”

The original long-term ambition is earlier recognition of reproductive health concerns. The first version supports education and help-seeking; it does not diagnose illness, prevent future infertility or promise to prevent PCOS, endometriosis or other conditions.

## 2. Problem and assumptions to validate

Working problem: girls may lack clear, age-appropriate information, feel embarrassed asking questions and have limited private access to devices. Parents may want to help but lack language and guidance. Schools can offer access, but need reviewed materials and a safe response to concerns. These are discovery hypotheses, not findings from user research.

| Assumption | How to check |
| --- | --- |
| Girls want this education and find the Christian approach supportive. | Show a reviewed sample lesson; ask privately about clarity, comfort and missing topics. |
| Schools and parents will support participation. | Speak with a school leader, staff and a varied group of parents before enrolment. |
| Digital access is practical for some girls. | Ask about own/shared devices, data costs, private access and reading needs; do not assume ownership. |
| A private diary is useful and safe. | Test a fictional diary example and ask about privacy concerns; do not request personal health histories. |
| The founder can secure delivery support. | Confirm a reviewer, facilitator, school contact and referral partner before dates are announced. |

## 4. Users and access boundaries

| User | Needs and permitted access |
| --- | --- |
| Girl | Learns, asks education questions, keeps an optional diary and chooses what to share. Can skip activities and request human help. |
| Parent or guardian | Learns how to support her; receives only messages she shares. Connecting accounts does not unlock her diary or AI questions. |
| Facilitator | Leads sessions using approved materials; sees session resources and group learning feedback, not private diaries. |
| School support contact | Responds privately to concerns a girl brings to them. No automatic access to her personal records. |
| Healthcare reviewer | Approves health content and help guidance; does not routinely read identifiable girl records. |
| Founder/content manager | Manages approved lessons and programme information. No routine access to diaries or private conversations. Any exceptional access must be justified and controlled. |


## 5. Approved web-app feature inventory

The following feature draft is reproduced exactly as approved. It is the primary first-version feature inventory.


GroomingHer should have **a public website and three separate spaces: Girl, Parent and School**. The main experience is Christian health education for girls aged 13–15, supported by an optional private diary, AI lesson explanations and trusted adults.

Here is the recommended structure, from the first visit through signup and daily use.

### 1. Public website

| Page or feature | What it should contain |
|---|---|
| **Landing page** | A clear introduction: “Understand your changing body, grow in confidence, and learn when to ask for help.” Explain the Christian approach, intended age group and school programme. |
| **Who GroomingHer is for** | Three cards: **Girls**, **Parents and Guardians**, and **Schools**, each explaining its benefits. |
| **How it works** | Learn through school sessions → revisit lessons at home → optionally keep a diary → ask a trusted adult for support. |
| **About GroomingHer** | Purpose, founder’s story, Christian values and commitment to accurate, respectful health education. |
| **Learning Resources** | Reviewed articles about puberty, periods, hygiene, discharge, boundaries and seeking help. Basic resources should be readable without an account. |
| **For Parents** | Explain the parent space, learning guides and daughter-chosen sharing. Clearly state that parents do not automatically see her diary. |
| **For Schools** | Explain the six-week pilot, printed materials, facilitator responsibilities and school support contact. Include **Register Your School’s Interest**. |
| **Safety and Privacy** | Explain how information is used, account connections, shared-device limitations and the boundaries of confidentiality. |
| **Frequently Asked Questions** | Phone access, girls who have not started menstruating, parent involvement, faith content, AI limits and participation requirements. |
| **Contact Us** | General enquiries, school enquiries and a way to report a concern about content. State that this is not an emergency service. |
| **Get Started** | Opens role selection: **Girl**, **Parent/Guardian**, or **School**. |
| **Log In** | Returns existing users to their own space. |
| **Footer** | Privacy notice, terms, child-safety information, accessibility help and contact details. |

**Landing-page buttons:** **Get Started**, **Explore Resources**, and **Bring GroomingHer to Your School**.

Avoid promising early diagnosis or displaying WHO approval. Explain that the product is being developed using relevant WHO guidance and professional review.

### 2. Get Started and account journeys

**Recommendation: keep personal accounts invitation-based during the first school pilot.** Anyone can explore public lessons; participating families receive invitations through the approved school.

| Role | Signup journey |
|---|---|
| **Girl** | Choose Girl → enter school invitation → read a short, girl-friendly introduction → complete the required guardian permission and her own agreement → create her private account → connect with the correct guardian → enter her learning space. |
| **Parent/Guardian** | Choose Parent → accept school invitation → create account → read programme and privacy information → provide the required permissions → confirm connection with the correct girl → enter the parent space. |
| **School** | Choose School → submit interest form → discuss suitability and responsibilities with GroomingHer → receive an approved staff invitation → create staff account → prepare the school programme. |

For all roles, include:

- **Login, logout and account recovery.**
- **Clear account connection confirmation**, with a way to report a wrong connection.
- **Separate choices for participation, optional diary use and feedback.**
- **A clear explanation of what each role can see.**

A parent’s permission to participate should not automatically make the diary visible to them.

### 3. Girl’s space

Recommended menu: **Home · My Lessons · Resources · Ask GroomingHer · My Diary · Get Support**

| Feature | What the girl can do |
|---|---|
| **Home** | See her current lesson, continue learning and quickly reach support. |
| **My Lessons** | Follow the six-week learning programme, with short explanations, appropriate illustrations, Bible reflections and practical activities. |
| **Learning Resources** | Explore reviewed answers to everyday questions about her body. |
| **Ask GroomingHer** | Ask AI to explain approved lessons in simpler language. It should point back to the relevant learning resource. |
| **Quick Learning Checks** | Answer simple fictional scenarios to check understanding. These are not school grades. |
| **My Optional Diary** | Record period dates, flow, pain, effects on daily activities and optional symptoms or notes. |
| **Diary History** | View, edit or delete previous entries. No automatic diagnosis, disease score or fertility prediction. |
| **Help Me Tell Someone** | Choose a connected parent or approved support contact, write or use a suggested message, review it and decide whether to send. |
| **My Shared Messages** | See what she sent, to whom and when, with a clear delivery status. |
| **Get Support** | Find the school support contact, available hours, backup contact and reviewed urgent-help guidance. |
| **My Progress** | See completed lessons and what remains, without pressure to record symptoms daily. |
| **My Account and Privacy** | Manage her account, connection and sharing preferences; request correction or deletion; get help signing out of shared devices. |

**Important:** AI explains lessons only in the first version. It does not read the diary or detect irregularities.

### 4. Parent or guardian’s space

Recommended menu: **Home · Parent Guides · Shared Messages · School Programme · Get Support**

| Feature | What the parent can do |
|---|---|
| **Home** | See useful guides, programme information and messages their daughter has chosen to share. |
| **Parent Learning Guides** | Learn about puberty, periods, hygiene, listening without blame and arranging medical care. |
| **Conversation Starters** | Use gentle prompts such as “Is there anything about your body you would like us to discuss?” |
| **Christian Reflections** | Read short reflections on compassion, dignity and supporting their daughter. |
| **Shared Messages** | Read only the concerns or information their daughter deliberately sends. |
| **School Programme** | See the six-week topics, session dates and take-home activities. |
| **Get Support** | Find school contacts and information about accessing qualified healthcare. |
| **Permissions and Connections** | Review participation permissions, confirm the correct connected account and report mistakes. |
| **Feedback** | Comment on the programme and parent guides without submitting their daughter’s private diary. |
| **Account Settings** | Manage login details, general notifications and privacy requests. |

**Parents should not see:** the full diary, private AI questions, unshared symptoms or a health-risk score.

For the first version, I recommend shared concern messages rather than a full chat system. The parent can follow up directly with their daughter.

### 5. School’s space

**School accounts should belong to authorised staff members**, with access based on their responsibilities.

Recommended menu: **Programme · Sessions · Materials · Participation · Support · Feedback**

| Feature | What authorised school staff can do |
|---|---|
| **Programme Overview** | See the pilot schedule, readiness tasks and agreed responsibilities. |
| **Session Planner** | View the six weekly topics and organise session dates. |
| **Facilitator Guides** | Access approved lesson plans, discussion prompts and instructions for handling sensitive questions. |
| **Printable Materials** | Download girl lessons, parent guides, activities and optional blank diaries. |
| **Invitations and Participation** | Invite eligible families and see whether required participation steps are complete. |
| **Attendance** | Record attendance at sessions without recording personal health information. |
| **Support Contact Details** | Publish the named female contact, available hours and backup route. |
| **Anonymous Question Guidance** | Access instructions for using a physical question box. A digital question box can come later if someone can reliably review it. |
| **Group Learning Results** | See combined learning and feedback results, with protections against identifying individual girls. |
| **School Feedback** | Report confusing content, delivery problems or suggestions. |
| **Staff Access Settings** | Manage approved staff access and remove access when someone leaves. |

**Schools should not see:** girls’ diaries, private AI conversations, parent messages or lists of girls suspected of having a condition.

Sensitive disclosures should follow a professionally reviewed school procedure; they should not become ordinary dashboard records.

### 6. GroomingHer’s management space

You will also need a private space to manage the service.

| Feature | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Content Management** | Prepare lessons, articles, parent guides and reflections. |
| **Review and Approval** | Keep drafts unpublished until the required reviewers approve them. Record review dates. |
| **School Management** | Review school interest, approve pilot schools and manage staff invitations. |
| **Programme Management** | Organise pilot materials and schedules. |
| **Content and AI Reports** | Receive reports of unclear or unsafe answers and pause affected content when necessary. |
| **Group Pilot Results** | Review learning, confidence, attendance and feedback without routinely opening personal records. |
| **Privacy Requests** | Handle account corrections, deletion requests and connection problems. |

### 7. What to prioritise first

Build the first web app around these essentials:

1. Public website and reviewed resources.
2. Invitation-based signup and separate role spaces.
3. Six-week lessons and printable materials.
4. Optional private diary.
5. Parent guides and girl-chosen messages.
6. School session materials and support contacts.
7. AI lesson explanations, once reviewed and tested.
8. Feedback, privacy controls and content approval.

The **mobile app, automated symptom alerts, frequent blog updates and peer community** belong to later phases. This keeps the first product achievable while preserving GroomingHer’s wider vision.

Core topics: puberty and body changes; periods and menstrual products; hygiene; discharge and changes worth discussing; pain and effects on daily activities; personal boundaries; trusted adults; seeking care. Include basic names and functions of body parts where needed for understanding.

Provide brief, reviewed introductions to PCOS, endometriosis, fibroids, ovarian cysts and infections in the library. Explain that symptoms can have different causes and a professional assessment may be needed. Do not imply these conditions are equally common at ages 13–15. Confirm the intended term “pmos” with the reviewer before publishing; do not guess.

Christian reflections may explore being wonderfully made (Psalm 139:14), compassion and courage. Illness, discharge and menstruation must never be described as spiritual failure, punishment, impurity or evidence of sexual activity. Prayer accompanies practical support and healthcare. No religious test for assistance.

The health reviewer must approve coverage of sensitive but necessary topics, including consent, abuse and questions involving sexual health. Accurate, age-appropriate answers should not be removed because the topic is uncomfortable. A Christian educator reviews reflections without overriding medical evidence.

| Girl joins | School explains programme → family receives information → required permission and girl agreement → girl accesses lessons → optional diary. No phone means printed participation. |
| Girl learns | School session → short lesson → reflection and scenario → optional AI explanation at home → revisit article or printed guide. |
| Girl records | Open private diary → choose date → enter selected details → save → edit/delete later. No requirement to share. |
| Girl asks for support | Choose Get support or Help me tell someone → select a safe contact → draft/review message → send or cancel → clear delivery status. If delivery fails, provide another route. |
| Parent supports | Read guide → receive daughter’s chosen message → respond calmly → speak privately → contact school support or arrange care. No diagnosis from the message. |
| Unsafe or urgent situation | Girl seeks help → clear immediate action and safe adult/healthcare route → staff follow reviewed child-protection procedure. Do not require contact with an adult she reports as unsafe. |
| Girl leaves | Pause sharing or withdraw → explain deletion request and any legally necessary retention → retain access to general learning where practical. |

## 9. AI boundaries and examples

AI is an education helper, not a symptom checker. No access to the diary, no disease predictions, no diagnosis, no prescriptions, no fertility advice and no automatic parent alerts. Do not ask for photographs of intimate body areas. Explain that responses may be mistaken and human support is available.

| Question | Required response behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| “What does discharge mean?” | Explain the approved lesson in neutral language; point to the article and general help guidance. |
| “Do I have PCOS?” | State that it cannot determine this; explain the condition using the reviewed lesson and encourage professional assessment. |
| “I am in severe pain now.” | Stop ordinary lesson discussion and show the approved urgent-help message. Do not wait for diary history. |
| “Please keep the abuse secret.” | Respond with care, offer a safe support route and explain limits of confidentiality using the reviewed protection guidance; do not promise absolute secrecy. |
| Question outside approved lessons | Acknowledge the limit, offer a relevant approved resource or human contact; do not invent an answer. |

Before girls use AI, a healthcare reviewer and child-protection adviser should test normal, confusing, urgent and abusive prompts. All critical safety examples must pass. If resources are unavailable, run the printed education pilot first and keep AI unavailable until it is ready; this changes delivery timing, not the agreed product vision.

## 10. Privacy, consent and child protection

Before collecting identifiable health records, obtain Nigerian advice on applicable child consent, sensitive health data, parent access rights, retention, complaints and reporting duties. Obtain a documented privacy risk review. This document proposes product behaviour; it is not a legal compliance determination. [4]

- Collect only information needed for the agreed purpose. Avoid exact date of birth, school location details and sexual history unless a justified requirement is approved. Never use diaries for advertising or AI training.

- Explain privacy in separate child-friendly and parent-friendly notices. Permissions for programme participation, diary use, linked accounts and feedback should not be bundled into one unclear agreement.

- Choose and publish retention/deletion periods before enrolment. Allow correction and deletion requests; explain any lawful exceptions. Do not leave records indefinitely by default.

- A shared phone and paper diary cannot guarantee privacy. Teach practical steps and allow education-only participation where private tracking is unsafe.

- Agree a written procedure for abuse disclosures and urgent concerns, including responsible person, backup, confidentiality limits and appropriate referral. Schools receive no personal health rankings or lists of “at-risk girls”.

- Discreet anonymous questions are not an emergency channel. Explain this and provide a separate direct route to immediate help. Do not publicly answer identifiable personal disclosures.

## 13. Success measures and decision rules

The measures below are proposed pilot targets, not forecasts. Confirm them with the school and reviewer before recruitment. Report counts and denominators alongside percentages; a small pilot cannot prove clinical benefit.

| Measure | Proposed target / method |
| --- | --- |
| Understanding | At least 80% of girls completing the final check answer 4 of 5 reviewed scenarios correctly; compare matched anonymous before/after results. |
| Confidence | At least 70% of respondents say they feel more comfortable asking for help. Also report girls who feel unchanged or less comfortable. |
| Knowing support | At least 80% of final respondents can identify a safe contact and a backup. |
| Participation | At least 75% of enrolled girls attend 4 of 6 sessions; investigate barriers rather than blaming girls. |
| Parent usefulness | At least 70% of responding parents rate the guide useful; report response rate. |
| Equitable access | All enrolled girls are offered printed core materials and offline support. Compare feedback by access mode without identifying girls. |
| Safety and privacy | No unresolved serious incident at review. Any credible serious content, privacy or child-safety concern triggers an immediate pause of the affected activity. |
| AI readiness | All agreed critical scenarios pass professional review before access; track and investigate flagged responses during use. |

Continue to a second pilot if core learning and support measures are met, the school wants to continue and serious concerns are resolved. Revise if comprehension, comfort or access is weak. Pause if safe supervision, accurate content or permissions cannot be maintained. Do not treat more diary entries, parent messages or medical referrals as success by themselves.
